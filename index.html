Frequently asked questions

Isn't the hump just bone? Mine feels hard.

Do this right now: reach back and press firmly into the bump. If your fingers sink in even a little — that's not bone. Part of what's there may be vertebra, and we don't claim to change that. But in most women, the bulk of it is a cushion of tense soft tissue and trapped fluid the body piled ON TOP of the bone after menopause, stiffened in place. That soft part is exactly what the Cradle decompresses — and it's why so many women feel that spot soften within weeks. It was never "just how you are now."

I've already tried braces, exercises and a chiropractor. Why would this be different?

Because those all attack the wrong layer. A brace yanks your shoulders back — and pushes your head further forward, which feeds the hump. Exercises strengthen muscle, but the cushion isn't muscle. A chiropractor adjusts joints for $150 a visit, and the tension is back by dinner. None of them decompress the soft pad sitting on your C7. The Cradle does only that — gently, with the weight of your own head, every night. It's the layer nobody else touched, which is why nothing else worked.

How long until I actually see something?

Two different timelines, honestly: you'll feel the release in the first session or two — that warm loosening as the muscles finally let go. What you see takes longer: the area softens to the touch over 2–3 weeks, and most women notice a visible difference in the mirror around week 4 of daily use. That's why every order carries 90 days — triple the time you need to know for sure.

Will it all come back if I stop?

Here's the honest answer: anything that requires months of willpower comes back the moment life gets busy — that's what happened with your exercises. This is built the opposite way: ten passive minutes, lying in bed, doing nothing. Women keep it up for the same reason they keep brushing their teeth — it costs nothing to continue. You don't maintain results with effort; you maintain them with a habit you'll actually keep.

Is it safe? An inflatable one made me dizzy once.

Those inflatable "neck clouds" are exactly what we designed against: air chambers that wobble, overextend your neck and press near the throat — that's the dizziness. The Cradle is one solid, stable piece of medical-grade foam that cradles the base of your skull. Zero pressure on your throat or the front of your neck. Start with 3–4 minutes the first nights and work up to 10 — most women describe it as deeply relaxing, and many doze off on it. (Had neck surgery or a diagnosed spinal condition? Check with your doctor first.)

Do I need exercises, an app, or to lie on the floor?

None of it. No routines to remember, no screen, nothing to charge or strap on, and your bed works perfectly — no camping on a hard floor. If you can lie down for ten minutes, you already know how to use it. That's the whole point: the first solution that asks nothing of you.

What if mine is from weight, not posture?

Then you're describing exactly what the Cradle targets. The soft pad is largely tissue and trapped fluid — which is why crash diets and even injections leave so many women with the hump still there, and why it returns with a few pounds. Daily decompression works on that cushion directly, whatever the scale says. No extreme weight loss required.
