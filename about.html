Our story

It was never your fault.

Kalmely started with a sentence thousands of women have heard in a doctor's office: "It's just age — try to lose a little weight." Said kindly, maybe. But it sends you home with a problem that's real, a body you've started to hide, and nothing to actually do about it.

The thing no one takes seriously

The soft, tender pad that builds at the base of the neck — the one that makes you angle away from cameras and take your hair down the moment you see the back of your head — isn't a character flaw and it usually isn't "bad posture." For a lot of women it's built-up soft-tissue tension that gets worse with the years, with menopause, with days spent leaning over a phone or a desk. It's real. And it deserves better than a shrug.

So we built the opposite of a lecture

We didn't want another brace that digs in, another set of exercises to feel guilty about, or another gadget that ends up in a drawer. We wanted something you could actually keep doing: lie back for ten quiet minutes and let the weight of your own head gently decompress the base of your neck. No force. No cracking. No willpower required.

That's the whole idea behind the Gravity Cradle — gentle, sustained traction that's slow and passive by design, so consistency comes easily and the tension has room to release over time.
